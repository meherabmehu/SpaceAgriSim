# Phase 7 — ML Evaluation

Like-for-like comparison of four model families on every task that has measured
data. Generated by `ml/training/benchmark_models.py`; raw numbers in
`ml/evaluation/phase7_benchmark.json`.

Two rules are enforced in code, not just described:

1. **Every split is grouped by the biological replicate** — tray, pillow or whole
   experiment. An assertion fails the run if a group leaks across folds.
2. **Every learner sees exactly the same folds**, baseline included. A model that
   scores well on a different split has not been compared, it has been promoted.

The scores below come from this harness (200 trees, its own fold realisation), so
they can differ in the third decimal from the shipped artifact, which is fitted
with 128 trees on the same folds. Where the two disagree the artifact's number is
authoritative and both are kept.

Baseline `Dummy` (mean for regression, majority class for classification),
Ridge/Logistic (linear), RandomForest, HistGradientBoosting. No neural model —
see `PHASE7_ML_TRAINING.md` §4.

---

## 1. Headline table

| Task | Validation | Baseline | Best learner | Best score | Beats baseline | Flexible > linear |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lettuce fresh shoot mass (n=1648, 4 experiments) | leave-one-experiment-out | R² −0.054 | gradientBoosting | R² **0.880** | yes | yes |
| Mizuna harvest mass (n=14, 10 groups) | 4-fold GroupKFold | MAE 14.41 | randomForest | MAE **13.95** | yes (marginally) | no |
| Microbial load, **absolute** (n=123, 2 campaigns) | leave-one-campaign-out | R² −0.475 | randomForest | R² **−0.253** | yes, but still negative | no |
| Microbial load, **relative** (n=123, 2 campaigns) | leave-one-campaign-out | R² 0.000 | randomForest | R² **0.381** | yes | yes |
| Tomato flight/ground classification (n=36, 21 groups) | stratified group CV | macroF1 0.444 | **logistic** | macroF1 **0.861** | yes | **no — linear wins** |

Three of the five rows are the interesting ones, and none of them is the
best-looking one.

## 2. What each row actually says

**Lettuce (R² 0.880).** The strongest result in the project and the one with the
least to do with NASA: 1648 plants over 4 experiments of external WUR ground data
spanning an environmental grid. Four held-out experiments is still four groups,
so this is a *model of that grid*, not a validated predictor of a new facility.
Gradient boosting beats Ridge here because the response is genuinely
non-linear in temperature and PPFD — which is a finding about the data, and it is
only credible because the same harness shows flexible learners losing elsewhere.

**Mizuna harvest (MAE 13.95 vs 14.41).** A 3 % improvement on 14 samples across 10
groups. It beats the baseline; it does not mean anything. This model stays at
`baseline-only` in the registry for an independent reason — the target's
definition differs between arms (`DATA_HARMONIZATION.md` H1) — and a 3 % margin
would not have promoted it even if the target were clean.

**Microbial absolute (best R² −0.253, still negative).** The most important number
in this document. The best learner available — including a flexible one — still
cannot beat the mean when asked to predict an unseen campaign's absolute load. No
amount of tuning fixes this, and Phase 7 reports it as a property of the
estimand. This row is what justifies v2 existing.

**Microbial relative (R² 0.381).** The same learners on the transferable quantity
score positively on both held-out campaigns, and the permutation test puts that
outside the null distribution (p = 0.000 on both, 200 permutations). The
difference between these two rows is entirely the estimand, which is why
`PHASE7_ML_TRAINING.md` treats it as a re-scoping rather than a tuning win.

**Tomato (logistic macroF1 0.861 beats RF and GBM).** A 36-sample task where the
linear model wins outright. Reported prominently because it is the counter-example
that keeps the benchmark honest: "flexible learners won" appears in only three of
five tasks, and here the simplest model wins with room to spare. The earlier
Phase 6 artifact reported accuracy 0.917 / macroF1 0.916 for its own pipeline on a
different split; the numbers here are not comparable to it because the folds
differ (stratified group CV inside this harness, selection inside each fold), and
that difference is itself a reminder that a metric without its split is not a
result.

## 3. Transfer is the discriminator, not pooled CV

Pooled cross-validation and held-out-study evaluation disagree, and where they
disagree the held-out result wins:

| Model | Pooled CV | Held-out study | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mizuna-microbial-load-v1` | MAE 1.087 vs baseline 1.718 (clear win) | R² −0.55 / −0.58 | Win inside pooled data is not evidence of transfer |
| `mizuna-microbial-relative-load-v2` | beats baseline | R² +0.44 / +0.37, p = 0.000 | Transfer demonstrated on the re-scoped estimand |

This is why the promotion rule in `PHASE7_ML_TRAINING.md` requires positive R² on
*every* held-out study, not a good average.

## 4. Uncertainty, reported at its true width

v2's interval is a group-aware split-conformal 90 % radius over pillows:
**radius 1.108 within-campaign standard deviations**, empirical coverage
**0.849** on 18 evaluation groups disjoint from 17 calibration groups.

Coverage is 5.1 points below nominal, so the artifact marks the interval
*uncalibrated* and its status stays `experimental`. Both facts are published
rather than adjusted: a residual quantile computed on the same points it is
evaluated on would have produced a flattering number, and the calibration and
evaluation group sets are kept disjoint precisely so that cannot happen.

## 5. Failures that stay failures

Named so they are not quietly dropped between phases:

- **Absolute microbial load does not transfer between campaigns.** v2 works around
  this; it does not solve it. Any absolute prediction needs that campaign's own
  baseline samples.
- **`mizuna-harvest-v1` cannot be fixed by modelling.** The target's definition
  differs between arms. That is a data-harmonisation defect, not a learning
  problem, and no learner will resolve it.
- **The tomato model is a demonstration, not a mechanism.** 36 samples, labels are
  recorded environment, not measured plant performance.
- **No NTRS target produced a model.** Twelve measured targets, two of them
  replicated, zero trained — because none had a transfer population.
