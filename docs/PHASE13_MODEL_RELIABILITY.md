# Phase 13 — Model Reliability Audit

Artifact: `ml/evaluation/phase13_model_audit.json`
Script: `python3 scripts/audit_models.py --write` (read-only; exits non-zero on failure)
Tests: `backend/tests/test_phase13_model_audit.py` (11 tests)

**Result: 8 of 8 registered models audited, 114 checks, 0 failures, 17 documented gaps** — two of
them high severity and stated as limitations rather than repaired.

This audit does not retrain, rescore or "improve" anything. Every number below is read from the
artifacts that are actually served. A pass means an artifact and its record agree; it says nothing
about whether a model is good, and this document does not claim that.

## 2. What the audit verifies, and why each check exists

**Checksums recomputed from disk.** The registry records a SHA-256 per artifact. The audit
recomputes it rather than trusting the record, so a hand-edited or truncated artifact fails
`--write`. A test additionally recomputes every digest independently of the audit.

**No orphan artifacts.** Every `ml/artifacts/*.json` is either registered, an explanation file, a
documented non-model (`tomato_example.json`, a single sample saved by the trainer) or explicitly
superseded. An artifact that appears from nowhere is how an untracked model gets served.

**Provenance.** Every artifact must record its source study, method, training time and seed.
Validation must be documented — under `validation`, or under the equivalent keys a superseding
artifact uses (`validation`, `transferSummary`, `evidenceBasis`), because the question is whether
validation is documented, not what the key is called.

**Schema.** Regressors need features, a numeric target and a unit. Classifiers need features and a
class label set. This asymmetry is deliberate: demanding a numeric target from a classifier would
produce a permanent false failure, and the natural next step after a false failure is fabricating
the field to silence it.

**Metrics.** Every model reports measured metrics with no null placeholders, and every regression
model reports the baseline it must beat. `mizuna-harvest-v1` is the honest counter-example: R²
−0.333 against a baseline of −0.138, i.e. the model does **not** beat a trivial baseline, and the
artifact says so. It is served as an exploratory model with that number attached, not hidden.

**Uncertainty and calibration.** A regression model must carry an interval or an uncertainty
record. A claim of calibration must be backed by a measured coverage. No classifier here claims
calibration, and a test asserts the served catalogue never reports `calibrated: true` for one.

**Classification.** Exactly one classification per model, in the single place the API and the docs
both read, so the two cannot drift.

**Supersession.** `lettuce-earth-growth-v2` supersedes v1 and `mizuna-microbial-relative-load-v2`
supersedes v1. Both predecessors are still on disk, unchanged, and the audit compares their
`trainedAt` against the successor to prove they were not regenerated by the newer run. Deleting a
predecessor destroys the ability to reproduce an older result.

**Dataset safeguards.** The `phase11-duplicate-osd320`, `phase11-gravity-osd346` and
`phase11-ood-rejected` findings must still be in the evidence corpus, the OSD-320 finding must
still state its prohibition in words, and no artifact may mention OSD-320 without a
duplicate/independence caveat. Details in `docs/PHASE13_DATASET_PROVENANCE.md`.

## 3. Documented gaps — the part that is not clean

The audit separates correctness failures from gaps. These are gaps: real, reported, not repaired
by editing a validated artifact.

**High severity — classifier calibration is undocumented.** Neither
`radiation-quality-osd46-v1` nor `radiation-exposure-osd46-v1` states whether its class
probabilities are calibrated, and no calibration analysis exists for either. The served surface
does not claim calibration (asserted by test), and the research assistant answers "are the class
probabilities calibrated?" with the repository's actual position — that no validated
out-of-distribution threshold or calibration study exists. But a reader of the model card alone
would not learn that. **Remediation:** add a calibration statement to both model cards, which
requires a calibration analysis to be run first, not a sentence to be typed.

**Per-artifact licence not recorded (5 artifacts).** `lettuce-earth-growth-v2` records CC-BY-4.0;
the OSDR-derived models record no licence field. The upstream terms exist — OSDR data policy for
the NASA studies, the WUR dataset DOI for the external data — but they are not captured per
artifact. Recording a guessed licence would be worse than the gap. **Remediation:** populate from
the upstream data policy, per study, by someone entitled to interpret those terms.

**Source URL and source type absent on 2 artifacts.** `mizuna-harvest-v1` (OSD-655) and
`tomato-flight-response-v1` (OSD-767) omit both. The URL is derivable from the accession and the
audit prints the derivation rather than writing it into the artifact.

**Coverage scopes are within-campaign.** Measured coverages of 0.94, 0.96 and 0.99 are on held-out
groups from the campaigns that were collected. Two campaigns are not a sample of campaigns, and a
third campaign's offset must come from its own baseline samples. The artifacts already carry this
caveat; the audit restates it so it cannot quietly disappear.

## 4. What this audit does not show

* It does not show the models are accurate outside their documented domains. Every model here is
  `trained-exploratory`; none has independent-study validation.
* It does not show the models generalise to a new campaign, a new cultivar or a new platform.
* It is not a scientific review. It checks that claims are recorded and internally consistent, not
  that the chosen learner, features or split were the right ones.
* No molecular or radiation classifier here predicts crop yield, biomass, survival or
  productivity, on Earth or anywhere else. That is not a gap — it is the design, and
  `research.model_registry()["unsupportedTargets"]` names those targets as not modelled.
